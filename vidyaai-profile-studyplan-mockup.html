Physics · Unit 1 · Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's Second Law of Motion

🔥 Weak area · ~18 min

📘 Concept, in plain terms

Newton's Second Law connects how hard you push something to how fast it speeds up. The heavier the object, the more force it takes to get the same acceleration — that's the whole idea. This is the law that explains why pushing an empty cart is easy and pushing a loaded one isn't, even with the same effort.

🧮 The formula

F = m × a
F = net force (newtons)  ·  m = mass (kg)  ·  a = acceleration (m/s²)
Double the force, double the acceleration — as long as mass stays the same.

✏️ Worked example

Q. A 5 kg trolley is pushed with a force of 20 N. Find its acceleration.
1 Write the formula: F = m × a
2 Rearrange for a: a = F ÷ m
3 Substitute values: a = 20 ÷ 5
4 a = 4 m/s²

🧠 Try it yourself

1. A 2 kg ball is hit with a force of 10 N. What's its acceleration?
a = F ÷ m = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 m/s²
2. What force is needed to accelerate a 10 kg mass at 3 m/s²?
F = m × a = 10 × 3 = 30 N
